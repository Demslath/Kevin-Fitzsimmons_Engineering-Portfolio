ProfileBioresource engineering graduate, McGill University

Profile

My path to engineering began in professional kitchens, where I spent a decade before completing a degree in Bioresource Engineering at McGill University.

My work now sits at the intersection of water, soil and ecological systems. I developed the standardized field sampling protocol for an international stormwater research project, designed a zero-waste digestate management system for a whiskey distillery’s biogas facility, and built the photobioreactor used to validate its least-proven stage. That project received the national CSABE | SCGAB Undergraduate Thesis Award.

I am relocating to Vancouver to begin my career in environmental engineering, with particular interest in remediation, ecological and wetland restoration, and water resources.

Vancouver is familiar ground. I first studied natural resource management at UBC, on the unceded territories of the xʷməθkʷəy̓əm (Musqueam), Sḵwx̱wú7mesh (Squamish) and səlilwətaɬ (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations.

Digestate to Clean Water

An algae-based approach

A Kentucky biogas facility generates a nutrient-rich digestate that the local municipal treatment plant cannot accommodate. Our team designed a four-stage system that converts the entire stream into fuel, fertilizer and cleaner water, and I built the reactor used to test the final stage.

Approach

How I approach engineering work

Reproducible methods

At the Université de Montréal, I developed the field sampling protocol for BioReStorm. Research teams in Canada, Sweden, Estonia and France now apply the same procedure, allowing results to be compared across sites and climates. Results that cannot be reproduced cannot be defended.

BioReStorm fieldwork

Designs validated by evidence

My capstone extended beyond the process design. I designed, built and operated a six-vessel photobioreactor to test the stage with the least supporting data, and reported both what the results demonstrated and where they fell short.

Capstone design project

Composure under pressure

A decade in professional kitchens, including Vin Mon Lapin, now ranked No. 5 on North America’s 50 Best Restaurants, developed habits I bring to engineering work: thorough preparation, a strong safety culture, and the ability to keep a team coordinated under pressure.

Respect for Indigenous communities

I currently work at Resilience Montréal, an Indigenous-led day centre in Tiohtià:ke (Montréal) serving unhoused community members, many of whom are First Nations and Inuit. The work has reinforced the importance of listening, building trust, and following community direction, which is equally essential on projects that affect Indigenous lands and waters.
