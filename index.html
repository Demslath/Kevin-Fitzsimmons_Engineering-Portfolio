In brief

I came to engineering the long way round: a decade running kitchen lines, then a degree in bioresource engineering at McGill.

Now I work where water, soil and ecosystems meet. I’ve written the field sampling protocol for an international stormwater study, designed a zero-waste treatment train for a whiskey distillery’s biogas plant, and built the photobioreactor that tested its riskiest stage. That project won the national CSABE | SCGAB Undergraduate Thesis Award.

I’m moving back to Vancouver, where I first studied natural resource management at UBC, to start my career in environmental engineering: remediation, ecological and wetland restoration, water resources and treatment.

How I work

Three habits, and where they came from

Write it so anyone can repeat it

At the Université de Montréal I wrote the field sampling protocol for BioReStorm. Teams in Canada, Sweden, Estonia and France now follow the same procedure, so their data can be compared. Work that can’t be reproduced can’t be defended.

The fieldwork

Carry the design to evidence

My capstone didn’t stop at a process diagram. I designed, built and ran a six-vessel photobioreactor to test the stage we were least sure of, and reported honestly on what the data could and couldn’t show.

Ten years of professional kitchens, including Mon Lapin, now No. 5 on North America’s 50 Best Restaurants. Service teaches you to prepare everything before you start, to keep everyone safe, and to keep a team moving when the pressure is on.
