Digestate to Clean Water

An algae-based approach to zero-waste digestate management

CSABE | SCGAB Undergraduate Thesis Award, 2025

Biogas plants solve one waste problem and create another: digestate, the nutrient-heavy sludge left behind. We designed a way to turn all of it into products.

Our client, Biogas-ZE, digests stillage from a Kentucky whiskey distillery and wants to run with zero waste and net-zero emissions. The distillery’s wastewater alone is roughly 70% of the local municipal plant’s treatment capacity, so sending digestate down the drain isn’t an option. Over two semesters our team of four designed the full treatment train, then built and ran a lab-scale photobioreactor to test its final, least-proven stage.

The system

Four stages, no waste stream

Each stage hands the next a cleaner stream and leaves behind something that can be sold or reused.

  1. Primary separation

    A decanter centrifuge splits digestate into solids and liquid at 85 to 92% efficiency, with no chemical flocculants and so no extra waste stream.

    Out: wet solids, low-turbidity liquid

  2. Solids processing

    Vacuum evaporation dries the solids at lower temperatures than conventional thermal drying.

    Out: pellets for solid fuel or soil amendment

  3. Liquid processing

    Ceramic ultrafiltration removes particulates, then a fluidized-bed reactor doses magnesium to precipitate struvite.

    Out: struvite, a slow-release fertilizer

  4. Algae polishing

    Microalgae strip the remaining nitrogen and phosphorus, absorb CO₂ from the biogas, and grow biomass that goes back into the digester.

    Out: cleaner water, more biogas

The build

A photobioreactor to test stage four

Algae polishing was the stage with the least data behind it, so I designed and built a benchtop reactor to grow algae in synthetic digestate under controlled conditions. It went through three prototypes: narrow-band red-blue LED strips, then a rigid broad-spectrum light platform, then the final frame below.

Vessels
Six 500 mL culture jars running in parallel, enough for two simultaneous trials
Light
Full-spectrum T5 grow lights calibrated to 100 to 105 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at each jar
Carbon
Automated CO₂ delivery, timed to the light cycle, held in the range that boosts growth without inhibiting it
Frame
Aluminum rails and 3D-printed mounts with heat-set threaded inserts, modelled in Fusion 360

What we found

Results

We grew Chlamydomonas reinhardtii for ten days at three ammonium loadings (7, 14 and 18 mM NH₄Cl), tracking optical density, pH and orthophosphate daily across two trials.

  • 14 mMthe ammonium level that gave steady, consistent growth
  • 18 mMgrew fast at first, then stalled: a temporary inhibition
  • < 10 ppmorthophosphate by day 10, in every treatment of both trials

What I’d change. The commercial test strips read un-ionized ammonia, but at our culture pH almost all nitrogen was ionized ammonium, so the nitrogen readings weren’t usable. The next round needs a lab method for ammonium to close the nutrient mass balance.
